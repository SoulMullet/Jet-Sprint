# Summer Series ’87 — v0.19.0

This release extends the existing v0.18.0 game into four three-lap races and three long connecting rivers. It retains Rookie Run’s Kōwhai geometry and race behavior. It does not replace the input adapters, boat force integrator, camera controller, V8 synthesis, fonts, ballistic spray or tuning stores. The previous [Phase 1 report](campaign-phase1.md) is historical.

Timberline adds a forested multi-island course with crossing routes. Devil’s Bend adds technical S-bends, repeated junction visits and hairpins. Championship has the longest, most complex prescribed route, more spectators and racing banners. Their authored layouts are distinct; the 25%/50% difficulty figures are design goals, not measured human difficulty or mechanically scaled geometry.

River channel widths increase from 21 to 22.8 to 24.6 m, versus the old 12.8 m river. Smooth spline meanders, downstream current opposing travel, progressively stronger standing crests/foam, optional side ramps, physical off-centre rocks on Rivers 2/3 and bank-side outcrops establish progression. Bank foliage, timber huts, docks, original signs and spectator silhouettes use shared procedural assets. No paid assets or dependencies were added. Mean water elevation is continuous; there are no height steps between stages.

## Architecture and transitions

`campaign-data.js` contains plain deterministic course/river definitions in metres and seconds. `campaign-world.js` reuses the existing closed-course graph generator and open spline sampler. It builds one immutable indexed collision network containing all four courses, three rivers and destination staging inlets. Rendering uses bounded pieces of that same topology; collision never depends on a scenery job completing.

`CampaignTour` in `campaign-tour.js` owns **RACE → EXIT → RIVER → STAGING → RACE**, repeated three times, then **CHAMPION**. It reuses `Race`, `RouteNavigator` and the original `Campaign` gate/checkpoint/jump behavior. Environmental current and rock contacts are separate from the unchanged `stepBoat` integrator. Directional checkpoints, three-lap validation and the previous initial countdown remain.

On a valid race finish the controller freezes the timer, records the result/reward once and opens the real exit barrier. The navigator leads to its outlet. Crossing the outlet starts river timing/checkpoints. Crossing the last ordered river checkpoint updates the active course/race/navigator references and begins the next venue’s existing starting lights. The boat continues physically through the staging inlet. Red/READY crossings cannot advance race checkpoints or start timing; only a forward start-line crossing after green starts the next race. Prestart staging contacts do not contaminate that new race’s clean achievement.

Normal transitions do **not** recreate the boat, scene, AudioContext, cameras, wake or spray, release held controls, or write new boat position/velocity/heading. The main fixed-step frame remains responsible for integrating inputs, effects and audio. Existing world-space airborne particles continue independently. Lighting stays in the same scene. Explicit Restart/new-run selection and DEV practice shortcuts deliberately reposition; these are separate from continuous progression.

The title offers unlocked starting venues. Selecting a later venue prepares its scenery before enabling Start; the initial title is the only place a preparation wait is shown. New runs at later venues begin in their staging inlet with no prize credited. This is milestone resume, not a saved midriver boat snapshot. Restart returns to the current unlocked venue; restart from practice returns to legitimate Rookie Run.

## Bounded world preparation

`CampaignStreamer` separates geometry/assets from event and reward state. Its lifecycle queue deduplicates requested/in-flight work, retains pending jobs, handles failures/retry and disposes owned GPU resources. Tiles are **160 × 160 m**. The existing origin scene covers four fixed tiles; the active neighborhood requests 5 × 5 tiles, and the next venue requests 3 × 3. Limits are 49 streamed chunks on Low/Medium and 64 on High/Ultra. Unneeded distant tiles are safely evicted; revisiting rebuilds their deterministic scenery.

The next venue starts preparing during the current three-lap race, rather than waiting until river arrival. The next area remains requested during travel. A single module worker performs polygon unions, exact water/shore/land clipping, triangulation and water tessellation, returning transferable typed buffers. Main-thread jobs install those buffers, then vegetation and props separately. All water and bank unions are clipped after union against shared tile bounds, avoiding artificial channel end caps. Grass UVs use world coordinates. Shared geometry/materials are retained while owned tile geometry/instance resources are disposed. Streamed water is excluded from the existing planar-reflection capture; nearby scenery participates in the existing camera obstruction query.

Worker initialization, geometry errors and a 45-second timeout enter a clearly labelled simplified-water fallback. Collision/navigation/progression remain available; the fallback does not pretend that full scenery was prepared. **Pit Control → RACE → Debug Tools → Retry scenery preparation** restarts the worker and retries affected tiles without resetting the campaign or wallet. A browser test deliberately rejects the first worker module with HTTP 503 and verifies this recovery.

The stream budget is a resource-count bound, not a device-specific byte/FPS guarantee. Complex junction triangulation takes several seconds off-thread in this environment. Preparing during the race provides headroom; fast teleports, extreme settings, a slow/failed worker or constrained hardware can still expose incomplete/degraded scenery. There is no hidden interstage scene reset or loading fade.

## Wallet and persistence

`campaign-rewards.js` implements pure validated transactions; `campaign-progress.js` owns storage and migration. Stable event IDs carry monotonically increasing attempt sequences and paid watermarks, so memory does not grow with a transaction history. Only the currently active unpaid attempt may complete. A restart invalidates older callbacks; duplicate completion, overlapping gate callbacks and stale replay callbacks cannot pay twice. Standard completion prizes pay again for real replays; the listed clean prize pays once, on the first clean completion. An all-clean first campaign earns **$15,400**; a subsequent complete replay adds base prizes totalling **$10,250**.

A clean event has no grounding beyond the normal bank allowance plus 0.7 m, no collision impulse above 1.5 m/s and no respawn. Small recoverable scrapes can remain clean. Valid completions record wallet, lifetime earnings, counts, best time, clean-clear count, first-clear state and the claimed clean achievement. Currency and counters are validated before use; practice cannot mutate these records or unlock venues. The wallet has a separate balance and lifetime total so a later shop can spend funds without rewriting earned history; no shop is implemented.

The canonical key is **`jet-sprint-campaign-v2`**, schema 2. If absent, schema-1/2 data in **`jet-sprint-campaign-v1`** migrates additively, preserving milestones and legacy bests with no retroactive payouts. The old key remains intact and is never overwritten; an older v0.18 tab cannot erase the new wallet. New transactions refresh the canonical ledger before synchronous credit/save. A storage failure keeps the unsaved record active in memory, warns the player and exposes **Retry wallet save**. Reload cannot recover data that the browser refused to store; this limitation is explicit in the UI.

Game/physics/water/spray, audio, graphics, camera, input and preset keys remain unchanged. Same-origin updates retain their values; a build version is not a storage namespace. Tests cover migration, dirty/first-clean/replay cases, invalid values, stale/duplicate attempts, reload and storage failure/retry. No backend or tamper-proof online economy is claimed.

## Interface and developer tools

The existing MARINE-87 components, Russo One/Chakra Petch files and seven tuning departments remain. The compact journey module adds current event, stage out of seven, wallet, clean eligibility, next event and river timing/checkpoint progress. Nonblocking six-second rewards show actual solo time, personal-best status, base/clean prize and wallet, respecting reduced motion. Race timing/laps, navigation, speed/RPM and analog rails stay in their original HUD. Championship has persistent completion status and optional series results; it invents no AI positions. **RACE → Series record / wallet** lists all seven bests, clean claims and lifetime earnings.

The dynamically imported DEV panel selects any of the seven practice events, reporting course, race/countdown, laps, exit gate, river checkpoints, worker/cache state and FPS. Practice grants no legitimate rewards/unlocks. The module, controls and shortcut API are absent from the production bundle. Production retains read-only inspectors.

## Publication and remaining limits

The existing GitHub Pages workflow runs tests/build, deploys `dist/`, then **Verify actual published build and font files** fetches the real published manifest and hashes every served file. It requires the full commit and **`four-track-1987` / `wallet-v2` / `worker-tiles-v1` / `analog-input-v1`**. A successful local build alone does not prove that the live site updated; publication must be checked for the delivered commit in the final deployment-verification step.

Physical mobile performance is unverified. Low/Auto budgets, one worker, shared meshes, instancing and bounded chunks reduce costs, but the Three.js entry bundle and procedural mesh preparation remain meaningful costs. The optional GPU water diagnostic solver remains fixed at Rookie Run; later rivers use the retained procedural/HF surface with shared standing crests/current and CPU visual-hull/spray sampling. This is arcade whitewater, not a new streamed hydrodynamics solver. Jumps retain the existing lightweight vertical ballistics rather than introducing a 3D rigid-body hull. The first four venues are implemented; an eight-track campaign, upgrades/shop, fuel economy, podium ceremony and online features remain out of scope.
